The (Holy) Ghost Particles

Neutrinos are the oldest particles we can still find in the universe. They were created just seconds after the Big Bang, long before the first light appeared. Neutrinos are tiny and invisible, and they almost never hit anything. Because of this, they can pass straight through planets, stars, and even people without slowing down. Neutrinos move through matter without stopping because they almost never hit anything. They slip through solid objects as if they aren’t even there, which is why scientists sometimes call them ghost‑like.

Scientists have observed that, on very rare occasions, neutrinos will interact with water. If a high‑energy neutrino strikes the oxygen atom inside a water molecule, the atom can change, and the water molecule breaks apart. The hydrogen atoms detach, and the pieces can quietly reform as separate hydrogen and oxygen, or combine with nearby atoms in new ways.

Right after the Big Bang, the universe let out a massive breath of "ghost particles" called relic neutrinos. The expanding universe has stretched them out and cooled them down into a slow-moving, invisible fog that fills every inch of space. They are so ghostly and weak that scientists haven't been able to physically trap one in a lab yet, but they are an indirectly proven fact.

Trillions of neutrinos are passing through your body right now. They come from every direction, moving straight through you without slowing down. They’re so numerous that, on average, they pass through you with only a tiny fraction of a millimeter between them — far smaller than the width of a dime.

They wait silently, held in reserve for whatever final command the Creator may give when the time comes to pull the trigger again.

Relic neutrinos are the ancient particles left over from the first moments of creation — the spent shells from the universe’s opening shot.
