Chapter 4: The Fall

Fall From Grace — Stevie Nicks

Once the Creation Was Complete

Once the creation was complete and running at a perfect zero-error baseline, Satan went right to work.

The fallen rebel had already lost his high-ranking celestial position and his "El" suffix to become Lucifer. Now trapped inside the closed box of the material universe, he could not rip down the firmament or smash the planets. Instead, he targeted the human minds of the humans whose creation had ‘triggered’ his demise.

Genesis 3:1-5

“Now the serpent was more subtle than any beast of the field which the Lord God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said Ye shall not eat of any tree of the garden? For God doth know that in the day ye eat therof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.”

The Oldest Trick in the Book

Satan executed a highly calculated deception. He did not use physical violence or terrifying monsters. He approached Eve with a casual conversation, sliding a tiny wedge of doubt into her thoughts with three simple words: "Hath God said?"

Then, he delivered the ultimate lie to entice them: You don't need to serve a Master Vintner. If you eat this fruit, you can become your own boss. You can be just like God. It was the exact same trap that got Lucifer thrown out of heaven in the first place—the delusional desire to sit in the center seat. By rebranding a simple test of loyalty as a divine corporate cover-up, Satan convinced the humans that the Creator was holding out on them.

Adam Follows

Satan targeted Eve first, using a conversational approach to alter her understanding of the boundary rules. Once the lie took root in her mind, the deception quickly spread to the rest of the team.

Genesis 3:6

"And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."

The Necessity of the Tree

The forbidden tree isn’t a cruel trap or a mistake left behind by a careless designer. It is an absolute necessity for freewill to be honored.

For a loyalty test to be real, the option to fail must actually exist inside the box. If the Creator built a universe where human beings were forced to always do the right thing, they wouldn't be free people—they would just be robots following programming. True free will cannot exist unless there is an alternative choice. It requires a real, physical opportunity to say "no" to the Creator.

By placing the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil right in the center of the garden, the Creator is actively honoring man’s free will. He provides the one specific spot in the entire universe where defiance is possible. Without that tree, the human choice to love and obey God would mean absolutely nothing, because there would be no other option on the menu. The tree exists because the Creator refuses to force anyone to love Him.

The Fall

The snake slithered lazily down a branch, catching Eve’s eye with a casual nod. "Hey guys, how's it going?" he asked, his voice dripping with easygoing charm. "Beautiful day in the garden, right?"

Eve smiled, adjusting a basket of gathered fruit. "Oh, hey. Yeah, it’s great. We're just hanging out by the trees."

"Awesome, awesome," the snake replied, shifting his weight. "Hey, quick question. Did God actually say you can’t eat from any of these trees? Like, the whole garden is totally off-limits?"

"No, no, of course not," Eve laughed, waving her hand. "We can eat from almost all of them! It’s just this one specific tree right in the middle. God told us that if we eat from it, or even touch it, we’ll die. So, we're just steering clear."

The snake let out a soft hiss that sounded remarkably like a chuckle. "Die? Come on, you’re not gonna die! That's a bit dramatic, don't you think? To be honest, God is just afraid of you."

Eve blinked, surprised. "Afraid of us? Why would God be afraid of us?"

"Because," the snake whispered, leaning in closer, "He knows that the second you take a bite, your eyes will be opened. You’ll be just like Him—knowing the difference between good and bad. He is terrified that you'll become His equals, so He’s totally gatekeeping the best fruit in the place to keep you under His thumb."

Why a Serpent for Symbolism?

The Forked Tongue: A physical symbol of duplicity and a split mind. He speaks with double meanings; never saying what he means or meaning what he says.

The Winding Path: Anatomically unable to move in a straight line, the snake must slither in a crooked zigzag. He cannot walk the straight narrow path.

The Dust Dweller: Trapped in the lower physical world, his movement forces him to crawl in the dirt – mirroring his low moral character and deceptive nature – while emphasizing that he will never actually ascend.

Eve looked up at the fruit hanging from the branches. It really did look delicious, and honestly, if God was hiding it out of fear, gaining that extra wisdom sounded like a powerful thing. She reached out, plucked one, and took a bite. It was sweet and perfect. She turned and handed it to her husband. "Hey Adam, try this."

Adam took a massive bite, chewing thoughtfully. Then, his eyes went wide. "Wow, this is amazing. Wait... why are we naked?"

"Oh my gosh, you're right," Eve gasped, suddenly dropping her basket. "Grab those fig leaves, quick!"

There was a frantic rustling of leaves as the two scrambled to stitch together some makeshift clothes. Later that evening, a gentle, cool wind began to blow through the trees. It was the Ruach—God's active, moving presence, stepping into the space to interact with them.

"Adam? Eve?" God’s voice echoed through the trees. "Where are you guys?"

Adam peeked out from behind a massive bush, looking thoroughly stressed. "Hey God! Yeah, so... we heard you walking in the garden, and we got scared because we’re naked. So we’re just... hiding out over here."

"Naked?" God asked, His tone shifting. "Who told you that you were naked? Did you eat from the tree I explicitly told you not to touch?"

Adam didn't hesitate for a second, pointing his finger straight at his wife. "It was her! The woman You put here with me. She gave me the fruit, so I ate it."

God sighed, turning His attention to the side. "Eve, what did you do?"

Eve immediately pointed downward. "The snake tricked me! He told me you were just scared of us, and he totally peer-pressured me into it."

God sighed, rubbing His temples as He looked between the two of them. "Adam, you followed your wife, so you're with her now, not me. You two have fun with your new pal snakey here. I'm gonna get going—I have some other creations I have to attend to. But here's something to wear. Forget about that light body you had before, here's a couple of meatsuits for you."

The Lord God made garments of skin for Adam and his wife and clothed them.Genesis 3:21

A Famous Hebrew Wordplay (Skin vs. Light)

Ancient Jewish sages and modern biblical scholars often highlight a profound phonetic wordplay embedded in this verse:

  • The Hebrew word for Skin is עוֹר ('Or, starting with the letter Ayin).
  • The Hebrew word for Light is אוֹר ('Or, starting with the letter Aleph).

They sound exactly identical when spoken. In Jewish midrashic commentary (such as traditions recorded regarding Rabbi Meir), it is suggested that before the Fall, humanity was clothed in "garments of light" (אוֹר) reflecting God’s glory. After they sinned, their condition shifted to mortality, and they were clothed in "garments of skin" (עוֹר).

With a sudden flash, their radiant, glowing forms vanished. In their place stood heavy, physical bodies made of flesh and bone.

"Hey, you look great, forget about those light bodies you had." God remarked, a hint of irony in His voice. "You look like animals now. By the way, Eve—I put a little monthly reminder in yours about what happened here."

God then turned His gaze to the snake. His expression hardened as He looked at the creature, seeing right through the scales to the former man he used to know as Uriel. God sneered, His voice dropping to a low, booming whisper. "Don't forget—I'll see you and all your friends at the end."

The snake swallowed hard, his bravado instantly evaporating. He looked down at the dirt and muttered, "Yeah, I know."

Without another word, God turned and left, ascending back to His domain completely above and outside the creation, leaving the garden behind.

The snake waited a moment, ensuring the divine presence was truly gone, before slithering back up the branch. He turned to Eve, flashing a confident grin. "See? He ran away. Told you He's afraid of you. One thing He was right about, though, is you two sure do look good in those new meatsuits. Especially you, Eve."

He leaned down closer to her, his voice dripping with charm. "How about you come over to my place tonight? I'll show you my new hot tub, and you can give me a closer look at that fine meatsuit of yours—and show me exactly how much of an animal you want to be."

Eve, having completely forgotten about everything that had happened just moments before, let out a soft laugh. She smiled, giggled, and blew him a quick kiss before she and Adam turned and headed off into their new world.

The snake watched them go, his grin widening into a sinister smirk as he called out after them, "Let's have some fun. We're running things now!"
