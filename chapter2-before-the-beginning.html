Chapter 2 - Before the Beginning

God Decides to Make a New Creation.

God decided to make a brand-new creation. He shaped the first man, Adam, out of plain earthly clay. Then, God called all the angels together in the heavenly throne room. He gave them a direct command: be of service to this newly created form of life—mankind (Qur’an 2:34, 15:28-31).

Now, before we look at what happened next, we need to understand what an angel actually is. Despite what many people think, angels are not robots. They have free will. They are essentially the servants and employees of God's massive household. When God assigns an angel a job, He gives them the exact high-level powers and abilities they need to get that specific job done.

Think of it like a business. While they are on the clock doing a task for their Lord, they are expected to focus completely and finish it to the best of their abilities. But when they are off the clock and not working on a task, they actually relinquish those special heavy abilities. They step down from their work duties and simply enjoy life in the heavenly realms where they live. And just like any business, some angels are naturally better, more reliable employees than others.

To make this service relationship easy to understand, think of a waiter at a restaurant. The waiter does not work for the customers. The waiter works directly for the owner of the restaurant. The owner sets the menu, and the waiter's job is simply to bring the customers what they want from that approved menu.

In this cosmic restaurant, God is the owner and the Chef. The angels are the waiters, and mankind is the table of customers. Humans are supposed to look at the menu God provided and make their requests.

But there is a strict rule: customers cannot treat the waiters like garbage. If a customer starts mistreating the staff, the Owner takes it personally and gets incredibly angry. In fact, the Qur’an warns that anyone who is hostile to God, His messengers, and His angels—including Michael and Gabriel—will find that God is hostile to them (Qur’an 2:98).

Because these cosmic waiters are constantly moving between the kitchen and the dining room, you never know when one might be standing right next to you. This is why the New Testament commands us to be kind to strangers, because you never know when you might be entertaining an angel in disguise (Hebrews 13:2).

Behind-the-Scenes Libraries

When you read holy books like the Torah, the Bible, or the Qur’an, you are reading the core, foundational text. But over thousands of years, ancient teachers and scribes wrote down massive secondary libraries to explain the hidden details, answer tricky questions, and record oral history. Here are the three main "behind-the-scenes" libraries used in this book:

The Midrash (Jewish): Think of the Midrash as a collection of ancient story-based commentaries. When the raw text of the Genesis story leaves a gap or skips a detail, the Midrash fills it in. It records the traditions, history, and deep meanings passed down by Jewish rabbis to explain why characters acted the way they did. For example, it is specifically written in certain parts of the Midrash that Michael and Gabriel's total loyalty was actually questionable at first—their active, powerful natures meant they weren't automatically immune to the temptation of pushing boundaries.

The Talmud (Jewish): If the Midrash is about stories, the Talmud is about the law, debates, and mechanics. It is a massive, multi-volume library where ancient sages argued over every single detail of how to apply God's rules to daily life. It contains deep discussions on everything from cosmic architecture to logic puzzles.

The Hadiths (Islamic): The Qur’an is the direct word of God, but the Hadiths are the recorded sayings, actions, and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad. They act as a massive historical archive. If someone wants to know how the Prophet explained a specific verse, or how he lived his everyday life, they look at the Hadiths.

Whenever this book uses an extra detail that isn't in the main scripture, it comes from one of these highly preserved, ancient historical libraries.

One high-ranking employee flatly refused to follow this new service order. His name was Uriel, whom we also call Satan or Iblis. He looked at Adam with total disgust.

"I am much better than him," Satan said, crossing his arms. "You made me out of bright, majestic fire. You made this creature out of dirty, ordinary mud. I will absolutely not bow down to clay" (Qur’an 7:12).

Satan turned his back on the throne. He walked away from God's light, proud of his own materials. He chose his own ego over his Creator's plan.

God looked at the remaining servants of the heavenly host. He turned His gaze toward Michael and Gabriel, narrowed His eyes slightly, and asked, "And what about the two of you? Where exactly do you stand in this rebellion?"

Michael and Gabriel exchanged a very quick, nervous look. They stood up straight, trying to look as innocent as possible.

"Oh, yeah, sure, Boss!" Michael blurted out quickly. "We are totally with you! No problem at all!"

"Yeah, completely!" Gabriel chimed in, nodding his head up and down rapidly. "Team God all the way. We would never rebel against you!"

God stared at them for a long moment. He looked completely unamused and highly doubtful.

"Yeah, sure..." God replied slowly. He sighed, knowing their wild, energetic natures would still require plenty of close monitoring down the road.

Then, a gentle angel stepped forward. It was Raphael. He didn't have a loud, fiery, or aggressive personality. He was a steady, quiet force with an ingrained fealty that stemmed from the fact that he was older than the others. This wasn't the first time he had watched a universe come and go.

Raphael looked up at the throne with soft, calm, ancient eyes. "You know me, Lord," Raphael said gently. "I will never defy you.”

God nodded slightly and replied "Yeah, I know."

The Servants

The ancient Midrash explicitly designates the specific tasks of the archangels. When discussing the inner nature of the heavenly host and their potential for rebelliousness, the rabbinic text focuses intently on Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael. Uriel is conspicuously absent from this specific teaching. His assignment was already permanently deleted from active service.

Michael's Mandate: Michael is the angel of cosmic justice and payback. Centuries later, Jesus perfectly defined Michael's role when He warned Peter: "All who draw the sword will die by the sword" (Matthew 26:52). This exact rule matches Michael's traditional symbols: the sword and the balancing scales. The scales weigh human actions, and the sword enforces the math of perfect justice.

Gabriel's Mandate: Gabriel is the angel of strength. When the prophet Daniel collapsed into a helpless faint from a terrifying vision, Gabriel was the worker who reached down. He physically gave Daniel the raw strength to stand back up on his feet (Daniel 10:18–19).

Raphael’s Unlabeled Antiquity: Raphael is not mentioned by name in the main scriptures. He is a senior worker whose existence stretches back before our universe even began. Because his tasks are too varied for a single label, he operates quietly behind the scenes. He acts as the channel for healing, water, music, and travel. He is also the conduit for the Holy Spirit. Raphael has an absolute loyalty rooted in massive experience. He watched the Chef build, run, and completely destroy entire realities before our current world was even drawn up.

God Realizes He Has a Rebellion

God realized He had a real rebellion on His hands. Satan was gone. Michael and Gabriel were acting nervous. Even the loyal angels needed a way to prove their true nature.

God looked down at the empty void below Heaven. He decided that this creation would also be a test. We call this place the physical universe.

God did not announce the test to the angels. He did not tell them it was an exam. He simply set the plan in motion quietly.

"I am building a temporary school down below," God told the heavenly hosts. "It will be a place full of pain, limits, and hard choices."

Then, God looked out into the darkness where Satan was hiding. God did not destroy the rebellious angel right away. Instead, God quietly wove Satan's pride right into the hidden design of the school.

"I am giving you a limited delay," God called out into the dark. "Go down there. Try your best to lead the humans away from My Ways. Let's see who actually listens to you, and who stays true to Me. But there’s a condition. You are not allowed to bother with any of those who remain loyal to Me."

The stage was set. The angels took their positions. The ultimate cosmic test was running, but nobody knew they were being graded.

Job as an Allegory of the Loyalty Test

The story of Job is about loyalty when everything familiar disappears. Job begins as a faithful man who lives a good and honest life. He has family, wealth, and respect. Then all of it is taken away. His world collapses in a single wave of loss. The test begins when Job has nothing left to hold onto except his trust in God. His suffering becomes the “reality test,” showing whether his loyalty is real or only tied to comfort and prosperity.

Three friends come to explain his pain, and their names match their attitudes.

Eliphaz believes that wealth is a sign of God’s favor. His name can mean “My God is gold,” and he acts like it. He thinks Job must have sinned because Job lost everything. Eliphaz represents the idea that loyalty is proven by prosperity. When prosperity fails, he assumes loyalty has failed too.

Bildad is the “son of contention.” He argues that suffering is always punishment. He treats Job’s pain like a legal case. Bildad represents the belief that loyalty is measured by strict justice. If Job suffers, he must be guilty.

Zophar speaks fast and harsh. He says Job deserves even worse. Zophar represents the belief that loyalty collapses under pressure and that Job’s questions show pride. He thinks loyalty means silence, not honesty.

Their voices form the human side of the test. Each one tries to push Job into admitting guilt or giving up. But Job refuses to lie. He stays loyal even when he does not understand what is happening. His loyalty is not based on reward, reputation, or comfort. It is based on truth.

Then Elihu appears. His name means “My God is He.” He acts like an Elijah type figure. He is not part of the three friend cycle. He speaks with a different spirit. Elihu says suffering can teach and guide. He prepares the way for God’s voice, just as Elijah often prepares the way for God’s action. Elihu represents the moment when the test shifts from human judgment to divine perspective.

Finally, God speaks to Job. God does not explain the reason for Job’s suffering. Instead, God shows Job the size and complexity of creation. Job realizes that loyalty is measured not by understanding but by trust. Job stays faithful even without answers.

In the end, God restored everything to Job double. His victory was his loyalty. He stayed true when wealth failed, when the accusers accused him, and when silence lasted long. His loyalty held through the reality test.

Satan is Cast Out

God did not waste any time. He immediately evicted Satan from the heavenly heights.

"Get out of here!" God commanded. "You are out. Hell is officially guaranteed for you and anyone who follows your footsteps."

Satan was absolutely furious. His pride was deeply wounded. He looked down at the little mud creature, Adam, who had triggered his entire downfall.

Out of pure, burning spite for mankind, Satan turned back to face the throne one last time.

"Oh, I see how it is," Satan hissed. "Since You love them so much, I am going to take them all to Hell with me! I will lie in ambush for them on Your straight path. I will attack them from the front, from the back, from the right, and from the left. Let's see how much they love You back!" (Qur’an 7:16-17).

It is a profound structural fact that this exact courtroom scene—Satan's proud refusal to bow and his bitter vow to ambush humanity—is repeated exactly seven times throughout the Qur’an (Qur’an 2:34, 7:11-18, 15:28-44, 17:61-65, 18:50, 20:116, 38:71-85). In ancient scripture, seven is the absolute number for divine completion and structural seals. The Creator did not repeat this courtroom logging by accident. He stamped this corporate mutiny into the system ledger seven distinct times to ensure that no human being inside the incubator could ever misunderstand the exact parameters of the test.

God looked down at the angry, fallen angel. He wasn't worried at all. In fact, this fit perfectly into His ultimate plan to run this strict loyalty and fealty test for everyone.

"Go ahead," God agreed calmly. "The test is on. Try your absolute hardest. Drag down whoever you can with your voice. Gather your forces against them. But I promise you this," God warned. "You will have absolutely no power over My true servants (Qur’an 17:65). Those who stay loyal to Me will see right through your tricks. And as for you and the rest who follow you? I will fill Hell with all of you together on resurrection day."

With that final word, Satan snapped out of the heavenly presence, plunging downward to set up his traps. Unlike modern biblical mythology – there was no war, there was no struggle. At God’s Command – satan was gone.

Uriel Loses His Suffix

Before his massive fall, Satan had a completely different name in heaven. He was known as Uriel.

In ancient Hebrew, names carry heavy meaning. The letters "El" mean "God". Every high-ranking angel carried the "El" suffix at the end of their name. It was like a holy security badge. It proved they were connected directly to the Source of all power.

  • Micha-el means "Who is like God?"
  • Gabri-el means "Strength of God"
  • Rapha-el means "Healing of God"
  • Uri-el meant "Light of God"

But when Uriel packed his bags and rebelled, something critical happened to his identity. By turning his back on the throne, he cut himself off from the Source.

"You don't get to wear My name anymore," God decreed as the angel fell.

With that, Uriel instantly lost his "El" designation. His holy security badge was completely deactivated.

Because he was no longer connected to God's light, his old name didn't fit him anymore. He fell through the dimensions and re-initialized under a brand-new title: Lucifer (Isaiah 14:12).

Lucifer means "the morning star" or "the bearer of light." But without the "El" at the end, it was a hollow title. He was no longer a radiant being reflecting the Creator's true light. He was just a wandering spark falling fast into outer darkness, ready to set up his traps for humanity.

The Bright Appearance is a Cheap Costume

An old quote from the scriptures warns us that "Satan masquerades as an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14). But now you know the real truth.

He isn't an angel anymore.

He lost his badge. He lost his "El" connection. He is completely unplugged from the Source.

When he shows up on Earth trying to look bright and beautiful, it is just a cheap costume. He is simply playing a character from his old job. He wears a fake plastic mask of his old Uriel uniform to trick humans into failing the loyalty and fealty test.

The Strategy of Camouflage and Deceit

Let's be completely realistic here. Satan knows exactly how the game is played. .

He knows he cannot get you to follow him to Hell by being obvious. He is not going to pop up in front of you, make an angry face, and yell, "Grrrr! I am Satan, and I am taking you to Hell with me!".

If he did that, you would run straight back to God. You would pass the loyalty and fealty test instantly.

So, he does the only thing he can do. He lies.

He uses his fake light costume to make bad things look good, and good things look boring. He makes pride look like strength, and selfishness look like freedom. He whispers gentle, comforting deceits into your ear, trying to make the path to decay look like the best ride in town.

Satan Deploys Quiet Traps

Satan doesn't force you to do anything. He doesn't have that kind of power. Instead, he sets quiet traps.

He studies you closely to find out what you love. Then, he plays directly to your personal desires. He figures out your weaknesses—whether it is money, fame, or just being right all the time—and uses them as bait.

He acts like a bad friend who always tells you exactly what you want to hear. If you want to make a selfish choice, he whispers, "Go ahead, you deserve it." If you want to hold a grudge, he tells you, "You have every right to be angry."

By agreeing with your worst instincts, he coaxes you right along. He makes you think you are following your own mind, when he is actually leading you straight to Hell with him, completely failing the Creator's loyalty and fealty test.
