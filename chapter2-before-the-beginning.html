God Decides to Make a New Creation.

God decided to make a brand-new creation. He shaped the first man, Adam, out of plain earthly clay. Then, God called all the angels together in the heavenly throne room. He gave them a direct command: be of service to this newly created form of life—mankind (Qur’an 2:34, 15:28-31).

Now, before we look at what happened next, we need to understand what an angel actually is. Despite what many people think, angels are not robots. They have free will. They are essentially the servants and employees of God's massive household. When God assigns an angel a job, He gives them the exact high-level powers and abilities they need to get that specific job done.

Think of it like a business. While they are on the clock doing a task for their Lord, they are expected to focus completely and finish it to the best of their abilities. But when they are off the clock and not working on a task, they actually relinquish those special heavy abilities. They step down from their work duties and simply enjoy life in the heavenly realms where they live. And just like any business, some angels are naturally better, more reliable employees than others.

To make this service relationship easy to understand, think of a waiter at a restaurant. The waiter does not work for the customers. The waiter works directly for the owner of the restaurant. The owner sets the menu, and the waiter's job is simply to bring the customers what they want from that approved menu.

In this cosmic restaurant, God is the owner and the Chef. The angels are the waiters, and mankind is the table of customers. Humans are supposed to look at the menu God provided and make their requests.

But there is a strict rule: customers cannot treat the waiters like garbage. If a customer starts mistreating the staff, the Owner takes it personally and gets incredibly angry. In fact, the

Qur’an warns that anyone who is hostile to God, His messengers, and His angels—including Michael and Gabriel—will find that God is hostile to them (Qur’an 2:98).

Because these cosmic waiters are constantly moving between the kitchen and the dining room, you never know when one might be standing right next to you. This is why the New Testament commands us to be kind to strangers, because you never know when you might be entertaining an angel in disguise (Hebrews 13:2).
