God Decides to Make a New Creation.

God decided to make a brand-new creation. He shaped the first man, Adam, out of plain earthly clay. Then, God called all the angels together in the heavenly throne room. He gave them a direct command: be of service to this newly created form of life—mankind (Qur’an 2:34, 15:28-31).

Now, before we look at what happened next, we need to understand what an angel actually is. Despite what many people think, angels are not robots. They have free will. They are essentially the servants and employees of God's massive household. When God assigns an angel a job, He gives them the exact high-level powers and abilities they need to get that specific job done.

Think of it like a business. While they are on the clock doing a task for their Lord, they are expected to focus completely and finish it to the best of their abilities. But when they are off the clock and not working on a task, they actually relinquish those special heavy abilities. They step down from their work duties and simply enjoy life in the heavenly realms where they live. And just like any business, some angels are naturally better, more reliable employees than others.

To make this service relationship easy to understand, think of a waiter at a restaurant. The waiter does not work for the customers. The waiter works directly for the owner of the restaurant. The owner sets the menu, and the waiter's job is simply to bring the customers what they want from that approved menu.

In this cosmic restaurant, God is the owner and the Chef. The angels are the waiters, and mankind is the table of customers. Humans are supposed to look at the menu God provided and make their requests.

But there is a strict rule: customers cannot treat the waiters like garbage. If a customer starts mistreating the staff, the Owner takes it personally and gets incredibly angry. In fact, the

Qur’an warns that anyone who is hostile to God, His messengers, and His angels—including Michael and Gabriel—will find that God is hostile to them (Qur’an 2:98).

Because these cosmic waiters are constantly moving between the kitchen and the dining room, you never know when one might be standing right next to you. This is why the New Testament commands us to be kind to strangers, because you never know when you might be entertaining an angel in disguise (Hebrews 13:2).

Behind-the-Scenes Libraries

When you read holy books like the Torah, the Bible, or the Qur’an, you are reading the core, foundational text. But over thousands of years, ancient teachers and scribes wrote down massive secondary libraries to explain the hidden details, answer tricky questions, and record oral history. Here are the three main "behind-the-scenes" libraries used in this book:

The Midrash (Jewish): Think of the Midrash as a collection of ancient story-based commentaries. When the raw text of the Genesis story leaves a gap or skips a detail, the Midrash fills it in. It records the traditions, history, and deep meanings passed down by Jewish rabbis to explain why characters acted the way they did. For example, it is specifically written in certain parts of the Midrash that Michael and Gabriel's total loyalty was actually questionable at first—their active, powerful natures meant they weren't automatically immune to the temptation of pushing boundaries.

The Talmud (Jewish): If the Midrash is about stories, the Talmud is about the law, debates, and mechanics. It is a massive, multi-volume library where ancient sages argued over every single detail of how to apply God's rules to daily life. It contains deep discussions on everything from cosmic architecture to logic puzzles.

The Hadiths (Islamic): The Qur’an is the direct word of God, but the Hadiths are the recorded sayings, actions, and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad. They act as a massive historical archive. If someone wants to know how the Prophet explained a specific verse, or how he lived his everyday life, they look at the Hadiths.

Whenever this book uses an extra detail that isn't in the main scripture, it comes from one of these highly preserved, ancient historical libraries.

One high-ranking employee flatly refused to follow this new service order. His name was Uriel, whom we also call Satan or Iblis. He looked at Adam with total disgust.

"I am much better than him," Satan said, crossing his arms. "You made me out of bright, majestic fire. You made this creature out of dirty, ordinary mud. I will absolutely not bow down to clay" (Qur’an 7:12).

Satan turned his back on the throne. He walked away from God's light, proud of his own materials. He chose his own ego over his Creator's plan.

God looked at the remaining servants of the heavenly host. He turned His gaze toward Michael and Gabriel, narrowed His eyes slightly, and asked, "And what about the two of you? Where exactly do you stand in this rebellion?"

Michael and Gabriel exchanged a very quick, nervous look. They stood up straight, trying to look as innocent as possible.

"Oh, yeah, sure, Boss!" Michael blurted out quickly. "We are totally with you! No problem at all!"

"Yeah, completely!" Gabriel chimed in, nodding his head up and down rapidly. "Team God all the way. We would never rebel against you!"

God stared at them for a long moment. He looked completely unamused and highly doubtful.

"Yeah, sure..." God replied slowly. He sighed, knowing their wild, energetic natures would still require plenty of close monitoring down the road.

Then, a gentle angel stepped forward. It was Raphael. He didn't have a loud, fiery, or aggressive personality. He was a steady, quiet force with an ingrained fealty that stemmed from the fact that he was older than the others. This wasn't the first time he had watched a universe come and go.

Raphael looked up at the throne with soft, calm, ancient eyes. "You know me, Lord," Raphael said gently. "I will never defy you.”

God nodded slightly and replied "Yeah, I know."

The Servants

The ancient Midrash explicitly designates the specific tasks of the archangels. When discussing the inner nature of the heavenly host and their potential for rebelliousness, the rabbinic text focuses intently on Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael. Uriel is conspicuously absent from this specific teaching. His assignment was already permanently deleted from active service.

Michael's Mandate: Michael is the angel of cosmic justice and payback. Centuries later, Jesus perfectly defined Michael's role when He warned Peter: "All who draw the sword will die by the sword" (Matthew 26:52). This exact rule matches Michael's traditional symbols: the sword and the balancing scales. The scales weigh human actions, and the sword enforces the math of perfect justice.

Gabriel's Mandate: Gabriel is the angel of strength. When the prophet Daniel collapsed into a helpless faint from a terrifying vision, Gabriel was the worker who reached down. He physically gave Daniel the raw strength to stand back up on his feet (Daniel 10:18–19).

Raphael’s Unlabeled Antiquity: Raphael is not mentioned by name in the main scriptures. He is a senior worker whose existence stretches back before our universe even began. Because his tasks are too varied for a single label, he operates quietly behind the scenes. He acts as the channel for healing, water, music, and travel. He is also the conduit for the Holy Spirit. Raphael has an absolute loyalty rooted in massive experience. He watched the Chef build, run, and completely destroy entire realities before our current world was even drawn up.
