The Axiom

The Constant Factor: The Absolute Perfection of God Almighty

Before you process a single idea in this book, you must establish one non‑negotiable axiom—the constant factor that never changes.

God Almighty always has been, always is, and always will be perfect. He is Omnipotent, possessing absolute and unlimited power. He is Omniscient, possessing infinite knowledge and Infinite Foresight across all past, present, and future timelines. His existence cannot be comprehended or measured.

From this absolute perfection, He creates. Not out of need, lack, or deficiency. He does not require a universe, nor does He depend on anything within it. Creation is an intentional act—an expression of His omnipotence, His omniscience, and His sovereign will.

This axiom is the anchor of the entire book. If a passage of scripture appears contradictory, or if any phase of creation seems flawed, the error never lies with the Source. The error lies entirely in human limitation. Once His perfection is accepted as the baseline constant, every element of the universe aligns with precise, cohesive logic.

He is that He is (Exodus 3:14).

It is also important to understand that this is not the only universe He has created, nor the only people He has formed. Creation is not a singular event but an expression of His will — and, He has been creating since forever.

The Role of Free Will

From His perfection flows another constant: free will. God hopes for the best for every soul He creates. He guides, instructs, warns, and illuminates the path toward a better future. But He never forces obedience. He never overrides choice. Free will is real, and therefore the consequences of free will are real. Hope does not override choice; guidance does not eliminate desire.

Throughout this book, you will see the same pattern repeated across every generation, every story, and every timeline: humanity consistently refuses His guidance and chooses immediate desires instead. This pattern does not reveal a flaw in God. It reveals the depth of human autonomy and the seriousness of free will itself.

The axiom is simple: God is perfect. Humanity is free. The pattern of history is the collision between those two truths. Every chapter that follows demonstrates how the perfect constant meets the imperfect variable.
