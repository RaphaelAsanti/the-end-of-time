  2. Whether or not you are saved by your belief in Jesus alone—with no other requirements—is strictly between you and Jesus. You do not need to convince me about your personal relationship with him. As for me? Absolutely – I believe in Jesus also. So, if you’re theology is correct, we’ll see each other in paradise. If your theology is incorrect then… Either way – it’s irrelevant.

This book is about examining the structural laws, the mechanics, and the legal blueprints of the covenant. When we look at Jesus through this specific lens, we are not looking at a religious icon. We are looking at his literal, recorded actions and words. We are tracking a precise legal strategy that he executed within a compromised, occupied system. By stepping away from standard church debates, we can finally see the undercover operation exactly as it occurred

Jesus comes along and finds Herod, the Babylonian puppet king who immediately wants him dead. He also finds an Israel that has traded the protection of God for the protection of Rome.

The religious system was running on a hollow frame. The grand Temple built by King Herod looked magnificent on the outside. But its beating heart was completely missing.

The Ark of the Covenant was gone. It had been lost centuries earlier during the Babylonian exile.

Because the Ark was gone, the golden presence of God was no longer carried on the shoulders of the Levites into the light of day. Instead, the system had relegated the divine presence to a pitch-black, silent room at the back of the Temple: The Holy of Holies.

Inside that room, there was no gold box. There were no carved angels. There was only a single, bare foundation stone sitting on a dusty floor. The physical throne of the Creator had been replaced by an empty, terrifying void.

The Ritual of Fear

The system was terrified of this empty room. No common citizen could ever look inside. Only one man on Earth—the High Priest—was permitted to cross the heavy veil. And he could only do it once a year on the Day of Atonement.

The preparation for this single moment was extreme:

  • The Isolation: The High Priest had to undergo an intense, multi-week purification ritual just to prepare his body and mind.
  • The Panic: On the final day, he did not walk into the room with confidence. He would quickly dash in and dash out, frantically sprinkling blood on the bare stone where the Ark used to be, begging for national forgiveness.
  • The Safety Rope: According to ancient temple tradition, the priests took a final, desperate precaution. They tied a rope with bells around the High Priest’s leg.

The rules of the incubator were absolute. If the High Priest entered the empty void with a single unconfessed sin or a corrupt heart, the unshielded holiness of God would strike him dead instantly.

If the bells stopped jingling, the priests outside knew he had failed the encounter. Because no one else dared to cross the threshold to retrieve him, they would use the rope to drag his dead corpse out from under the curtain.

The system had turned the vibrant, moving force of the Creator into a hidden, terrifying death trap.

But Jesus had to go about His mission.

The religious leaders were looking for the promised "Elijah" and "Messenger of the Covenant" foretold in Malachi. But they seemingly forgot that Moses had already told of their failure. They were destined to be scattered across the earth instead of blessed. Because of this, a certain subterfuge had to occur.

Jesus could not just march in as a loud military conqueror to smash Rome. If he did that, he would have ruined the real purpose of why we are here on Earth. The rulers expected a visible king to set up an earthly empire. They did not realize that their nation was already marked for judgment and scattering. Because the people chose the safety of a pagan empire over God, Jesus had to arrive in total secret.

Likewise, Jesus couldn’t just go up on a mountain and declare himself to be the Messenger of the Covenant and change the rules. He had to complete his task from within hostile territory.

This need for secrecy shaped his entire journey:

  • The Quiet Approach: Jesus constantly told people he healed to keep it a secret. This was a tactical move. It kept him under the radar of Herod and Rome until his work was done.
  • Changing the Focus: Moses had already written down that the physical nation would fail and split apart. So, Jesus did not try to fix the corrupt government or the temple buildings. Instead, he shifted focus entirely to the inside of a person—their thoughts, motives, and heart.
  • Fixing the True Problem: The religious leaders were obsessed with outward rules and looking perfect. Jesus completely flipped this thinking. He showed that our physical bodies are slow and weak, but our inner souls are what matter to God.

By arriving as a poor carpenter’s son from a small town, Jesus pulled off a perfect hidden mission. He let the corrupt rulers think they were in charge. Meanwhile, he was quietly teaching people how to truly connect with God so they could survive the final judgment at the end of time.

The Sermon on the Mount: The New Covenant

Jesus couldn’t just climb a mountain and proclaim he was changing the rules. He would have been arrested and executed almost instantly. So, he began quietly changing the covenant right under the noses of the Pharisees.

The Pharisees were total experts in the fine print of the old law. They thought protecting the covenant meant guarding outward physical rules, like washing hands and measuring taxes on herbs. While they were obsessing over those tiny details, Jesus used the Sermon on the Mount to quietly shift the entire system to a new set of internal rules.

Some of the changes I’ll list here:

  • The Forgiveness Equation: When Jesus said, "You have to forgive if you want to be forgiven," he was replacing the old way of animal sacrifice to gain forgiveness. Under the old rules, you had to buy an animal, take it to a priest, and shed blood on a stone altar to get your record cleared. Jesus bypassed the altar completely. Your willingness to clear another person’s debt is the exact mechanism that triggers God to clear yours. The animal sacrifices were rendered obsolete.
  • A Gentler “Purging the Evil From Among You”: Jesus also changed how to handle wrongdoers. He commanded that you must go and give a warning individually. If they do not listen, you must send two or three people to give a final warning before excommunication. By requiring two or three people, Jesus was explicitly avowing and upholding the original Law of Moses—which stated that any matter must be established by two or three witnesses. He kept the legal structure of the law intact, but he changed the outcome. The warnings leading to excommunication were a "gentler" continuance of "you must purge the evil from among you." He swapped out physical execution for a verbal warning system.
  • Adultery Redefined: When Jesus redefined adultery to include looking at a person with lustful intent, he was initializing a fundamental change in how human output is tracked. Under the old rules, the system only logged physical, outward actions—you had to physically commit the act to trigger a violation. Jesus shifted the tracking coordinates entirely. He revealed that the New Covenant records the instantaneous thoughts and hidden intentions of the mind with the exact same weight as spoken words and physical deeds. The slow, outward physical body was no longer the primary baseline for evaluation; the lightspeed choices of the inner soul became the target of the audit.
  • The Consequence of Breaking The Covenant: To lock in the high stakes of this new universal agreement, Jesus established a stark, unyielding penalty for failing to maintain the covenant. He declared that believers are intended to be the salt of the earth, preserving the creation from decay. But if the salt loses its flavor, it is no longer good for anything. He explicitly decreed that the consequence for breaking this covenant is to be thrown out in the trash and trampled underfoot by men. If a soul compromises its core assignment and defaults back to animal corruption, the blessings from God are completely revoked, leaving the asset to be utterly discarded and broken by the lower elements of the world.

The Topography of Solitude: Mount Eremos

The covert, under-the-radar nature of this covenant change is not merely a thematic deduction; it is hard-coded into the literal geography of the event. The hillside where Jesus gathered the multitudes to deliver the Sermon on the Mount was anciently designated as Mount Eremos.

In first-century Greek, the word erēmos literally means a solitary, deserted, or unpopulated place of quiet. It represents an environment entirely removed from the artificial noise, political structures, and institutional oversight of localized society.

This choice of theater carries immense structural significance. Jesus did not stage this monumental systemic update in the crowded, hyper-visible courts of the Jerusalem Temple, nor did he use the formal town squares of Tiberias. Instead, he gathered the people in a location whose very name signified concealment and isolation. By establishing the parameters of the New Covenant on the slopes of Mount Eremos, he was ensuring that the transition occurred entirely outside the visual field of the religious authorities.

The Authorship of the Four Gospels

When interpreting the words and actions of Jesus, it helps to understand the various differences between the four gospels and the particular world-views of their writers:

The Gospel of Matthew: Written by an original disciple and direct eyewitness to Jesus’s ministry. As a former tax collector, Matthew possessed formal training in meticulous record-keeping and documentation. His account is heavily cross-referenced with Old Testament prophecies and laws, explicitly tailored for a Jewish audience tracking the historical fulfillment of scripture.

The Gospel of John: Written by a direct disciple, eyewitness, and member of Jesus’s inner circle. John completely bypasses the standard chronological framework used by the other writers. His text is written from a highly reflective, theological perspective, focusing intensely on the profound nature of divinity and the inner transformation of the human spirit.

The Gospel of Luke: Compiled by a travel companion to the Apostle Paul. Because Paul’s primary mission was preaching the theological meaning of the resurrection across the Roman empire rather than documenting daily history, Luke conducted an exhaustive, independent investigative report. He explicitly notes in his introduction that he interviewed surviving firsthand eyewitnesses to compile a highly orderly, chronological narrative.

The Gospel of Mark: Written by a first-century Jerusalem Jew who later traveled to Rome. Mark was not an eyewitness to Jesus's physical ministry. Instead, he served as the personal translator and scribe for the Apostle Peter, documenting Peter's direct memories. Writing from the heart of the empire, Mark formatted his text according to succinct Roman biographical standards, deliberately stripping away dense eastern genealogies to deliver a fast-paced, action-oriented historical summary.

Two Disparities and Their Resolution

John 3:16 is one of the most quoted verses in the Bible: “God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son.” Many people assume Jesus said this, but He didn’t. These are John’s words, not Jesus’s. This already creates the first disparity. In Matthew, Jesus warns that “Not everyone who says ‘Lord, Lord’ will enter the kingdom of heaven.” Matthew focuses on loyalty and obedience. John focuses on identity and relationship. They are not the same speaker, and they do not use the same language. The first disparity is resolved by recognizing that John is giving commentary, while Matthew is quoting Jesus directly.

The second disparity comes from the word “begotten.” In the Bible’s own vocabulary, “begotten” is used for offspring. In genealogies, “begat” and “begotten” always refer to children born into a family line from physical procreation. This is the language the reader already knows. So when John uses the phrase “only begotten Son,” he uses a word that carries the weight of lineage, offspring, and descent. Words matter, and this word has a history inside the Bible itself.

The Qur’an says clearly that “God did not beget a son.” This is a direct rejection of any idea that God produces offspring. It protects God’s Sovereignty and denies any picture of God having a child the way humans do. When you place these two statements side by side, the tension becomes obvious. One uses the Bible’s own offspring language. The other denies that God has offspring at all.

The resolution is simple once you take the language seriously. John is using “begotten” as part of his own theological vocabulary, not quoting Jesus. Matthew avoids the word entirely. The Qur’an rejects the offspring meaning that “begotten” carries in biblical English. So the conflict is not between Jesus and the Qur’an. It is between John’s chosen wording and the Qur’an’s rejection of offspring language. And this rejection serves as a reminder that the words you use matter, because careless wording can lead people into ideas about God that break loyalty to truth. When you understand this, the two disparities dissolve. Each text is guarding a different truth, and the tension comes from the word itself, not from the message.

  • The Diet Code Unchanged: Jesus proved that pig meat is still completely unclean. He did not change the physical diet laws of the universe. Instead, as the representative of Universal Truth, he clearly showed not only that pigs are unclean but that his words and deeds didn’t contradict themselves. If they had, he wouldn’t have been the representative of Universal Truth.

The swine incident can be read as a quiet confirmation of the original dietary laws. If Jesus intended to declare pig meat clean, this moment would have been a natural place to signal that change. Instead, the event unfolds in a way that preserves the earlier classification.

And because Jesus’ actions always align with his words and meanings — consistently, deliberately, and without contradiction — the way he handled the swine carries weight. This doesn’t demand a conclusion, but it does offer a meaningful detail for readers who are re‑examining how the new covenant interacts with the old one.

Resolution 1: The Meaning of the Words (The Language Option)

Therefore, the statement that "he was not killed by crucifixion" is medically accurate.

But when we look at the original language and what happened next in history, there is another possible translation. He may have actually said: "Father, leave them to their error, because they are ignorant."

Forgiven or Not?

So, another possible translation is:

"Father, let them continue in their error, because they are ignorant."

If you apply that translation, there was no blanket spiritual pardon for his killers.

40 years later Jerusalem was sacked and the temple was destroyed. That gives an indication as to which translation was actually applied by God.

it’s entirely possible He meant both at the same time — as you’ll learn in the final chapter of this book.

His visitation concludes not with a loud public ascension, but with a quiet, highly symbolic encounter on the shores of the Sea of Galilee. Here, Jesus prepares a breakfast of fish over a fire for his disciples. It is within this specific setting that he establishes his final, personal covenant with Peter, tasking him to lead the next phase of the network deployment.

The fish over the fire is a literal depiction of Nun-Shin (נ-ש):

  • The Nun (נ) Principle: In the ancient Semitic alphabets (both Hebrew and Aramaic), the letter Nun literally translates to "Fish" or a sprouting seed of life. It represents perpetual continuation, faithfulness, and the active preservation of life underneath the surface waters.
  • The Shin (ש) Principle: The letter Shin physically represents "Fire". It signifies the application of sharp pressure, transformation, the burning away of the old system.

By sitting on the beach, stoking a fire (Shin), and cooking fish (Nun), Jesus was quite literally enacting a physical code cipher before his disciples' eyes. He was binding the all-consuming fire of the Holy Spirit (Shin) directly to the enduring, reproducing seed of the human soul (Nun).

However, this display was also a profound, visual warning regarding the final rendering of the timeline. By handling the fish over the fire, Jesus was silently communicating a clear prophetic message to his disciples: "I will see you later, at the end of time, when God permanently separates the good fish from the bad fish."

This final covenant with Peter was the ultimate confirmation that the New Covenant was fully active—merging spiritual fire with biological life to sustain the human incubator, while serving as a stark physical reminder that a final, absolute sorting of the human ledger awaits at the close of the age.
