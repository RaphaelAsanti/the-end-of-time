Knockin' on Heaven's Door—Guns ‘n Roses

There’s a difference between how ancient cultures viewed God and time. When we study ancient polytheistic religions—the belief in many gods—we quickly notice something fascinating: they are all basically the same characters disguised under different names. Whether you look at the Greeks, the Romans, the Egyptians, or the Norse, their pantheons share the exact same archetypes.

The Bull

Across almost all ancient polytheistic cultures, the bull and the cow held ultimate spiritual importance. Long ago, before modern machinery, cattle were a family's most valuable asset. A bull represented raw danger and strength, and its deep roar sounded like thunder to ancient ears.

Because of this physical power, ancient societies universally used the bull to represent their highest thunder and storm gods:

  • In Greece and Rome: Zeus and Jupiter famously took the form of giant white bulls, ruling over the sky and throwing crackling thunderbolts down to the earth.
  • In Scandinavia: The Norse god Thor used a bull's head as hunting bait, controlling the crashing thunder and life-giving rain with his massive hammer.
  • In Egypt: People worshipped a living black bull named Apis as a literal god on earth, tying its strength directly to the annual flooding of the Nile River.
  • In India (Hinduism): The divine bull Nandi is worshipped as the sacred companion and gatekeeper to the high god Shiva. Meanwhile, the female cow represents a gentle, life-giving mother.

The ancient religion of the Bible stood in direct opposition to these animal cults. The most famous clash occurred when Moses went up Mount Sinai. Terrified that he was gone, the people melted their gold to build a golden bull statue. They reverted straight back to the comfortable Egyptian paganism they knew. When Moses returned, he crushed the statue into fine dust, threw it into the water, and forced the people to drink it. He did this to prove a physical animal has zero power to protect them, separating the physical world from the invisible Creator.

The Secret of Baal

The ultimate symbol of Baal is a bull. In ancient languages, the word Baal was not originally a proper name. It was a title that literally translated to "lord," "master," "owner," or "husband".

This etymology ties directly back to the original fall of Satan. When Lucifer rebelled in the heavenly boardroom, his core sin was pride—the desire to sit in the center seat and be the ultimate "owner" of reality. By convincing humans to worship a bull—the ultimate physical symbol of brute power and mastery—Satan tricked humanity into serving a false master who claimed ownership over the earth's fertility and wealth.

In fact, this current physical universe exists because of and is a direct function of Baal worship. It is an environment built around the illusion of independent ownership apart from the Creator. This is exactly why Jesus flatly stated that this is "satan's world." It operates completely under the rules of the false master.

The “Husband” etymology is the reason God told Eve “Your husband will rule over you.” - that linguistic link connects directly to Genesis 3:16. When God says to Eve, "and he shall rule over you," the Hebrew text uses the verb root ba'al (בָּעַל) to describe that rule.

In the original Hebrew structure, this sentence marks a massive operational shift in human relationships within the incubator.

The Shift from Partner to Master

  1. Before the Fall (Genesis 2): The relationship is defined as peer-to-peer. Eve is called an ezer kenegdo—a helper corresponding to him, standing opposite him as an equal counterpart.
  2. After the Fall (Genesis 3): The introduction of the error (sin) breaks that symmetry. God explains the consequence using the word ba'al. The husband transitions from an equal partner into a literal master, owner, and lord over her.

The Devout Monotheists

While polytheism fractured the ancient world into thousands of competing idols, true monotheism—the belief in one sole Creator—united the major prophetic lineages. No matter your background, Muslims, Christians, and Jews can all agree that Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad were devout monotheists. None of these men pointed to nature, animals, or multiple celestial entities. They pointed exclusively to the single, sovereign God Almighty.

Circular Time vs. Linear Time

The primary difference between the Abrahamic religions and Eastern traditions like Hinduism comes down to how they view time.

  • Circular Time: Polytheistic cultures watched the repeating cycles of nature—the seasons, the sunrise, life, and death. They believed time goes around in a never-ending circle, which birthed the concept of reincarnation.
  • Linear Time: The monotheistic faiths smashed this circle and laid down a straight arrow. Time has a definitive beginning and a definitive ending.

Reincarnation in a Fallen World

To clarify this fully, let us make a flat-out, personal statement: I have distinct memories of previous existences here in this world. To some, this might sound completely contradictory to a straight-line, Abrahamic theology. It is not.

Reincarnation is not a cosmic wheel of eternal spiritual evolution, nor does it override the finality of biblical judgment. Instead, it is a built-in function of how this fallen universe currently works.

This linear structure can be understood as "time within God's time." Because of the Fall of man, humanity was placed into a temporary, linear bubble where our actions matter and our deadlines are real. Within this bubble, the Creator operates with a system ledger of absolute justice and supreme mercy. While humans experience hours and years painfully slow, the Creator exists completely outside this constraint. As scripture notes, a single day is like a thousand years to God, and a thousand years is like a single day.

Because a thousand years is a mere day to the Sovereign, the system can allow a soul to loop back into a new biological interface to face unfinished moral lessons, correct previous spiritual debts, or fulfill deep obligations within the grand layout.

However, these small loops cannot run forever. The straight line of history will eventually hit the end of the world. The Bible explicitly teaches that it is appointed for a person to die only once, and after that face the final judgment. When that grand finale arrives, the small loops stop spinning, the past lives are unified into the complete record of the soul, and every single person stands directly before the Almighty for their final grading.

The Ancient Maze: From Crete to Pamplona

The obsession with the physical power of the bull did not stop at golden statues. In the ancient Greek kingdom of Minoan Crete, this animal cult took on a terrifying form. Mythology records the story of the Minotaur—a monstrous half-man, half-bull creature hidden away inside a complex, winding labyrinth. The Greeks designed a brutal ritual where human sacrifices were forced into this dark maze. They were left completely trapped while a furious, charging bull hunted them down to trample and kill them.

This ancient layout of humans running for their lives inside a stone trap is not a dead piece of history. Centuries later, this identical ritual still triggers every single year in Pamplona, Spain, during the annual Running of the Bulls. Thousands of people willingly crowd into narrow, stone streets that form a literal physical maze. They flee in panic while massive, angry bulls chase them down the cobblestones. Humanity is still playing the exact same pagan game, trapped inside a circular trap of fear and survival, completely blind to the fact that they are repeating an ancient sacrifice to Baal.

The Jesuit Discovery: Confucius and the Log in the Eye

As the linear straight line of history marched forward, Western thinkers began traveling to the Far East to spread their faith. In the 1600s, Catholic Jesuit missionaries arrived in China. They were completely stunned when they opened the text files of Confucius, the central philosopher of Chinese history.

The missionaries expected to find alien paganism. Instead, they found ethical rules that looked almost identical to the words of Jesus.

  • The Golden Rule: Centuries before Christ walked the Earth, Confucius explicitly taught: "Do not do to others what you would not like yourself."
  • The Internal Mirror: Confucius taught a systematic framework for moral life. He stated you must first perfect your own inner heart, then perfect your family, and only then attempt to correct your neighbors.

This was a perfect cross-platform mirror of Christ’s command in the Sermon on the Mount: "First take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take the splinter out of your neighbor’s eye." The Jesuits realized that the Creator had dropped matching moral guidelines into separate civilizations to prepare them for the same baseline test.

Here are a few quick pairs of matching teachings you can decide for yourself how closely Confucian teachings matched the teachings of Jesus:

  • On True Treasures: Confucius taught that a person should focus on building inner character rather than chasing wealth, saying, "The superior man thinks of virtue; the small man thinks of comfort." This mirrors Jesus warning his followers to not chase earthly wealth: "Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth... but store up for yourselves treasures in heaven."
  • On Judging by Fruit: Confucius advised evaluating people by their real-world actions over their smooth talking, stating, "Listen to their words and observe their deeds." This matches Jesus's exact rule for spotting false prophets: "By their fruits you will know them."
  • On Self-Correction Over Revenge: Confucius taught that when people wrong you, look inward first: "When you see a good man, think of emulating him; when you see a bad man, examine your own heart." This beautifully mirrors Jesus's push for self-reflection over finger-pointing: "Let any one of you who is without sin be the first to throw a stone."

Moving Past Morals: Intrigued by the Tao

However, the deeper the Catholics looked into Eastern libraries, the more their focus shifted away from Confucius's social rules. They returned to the West carrying a massive, intense interest in a far deeper framework: The Tao (The Way).

Written by the sage Laozi, the Tao Te Ching did not focus on lists of active commandments. Instead, it described a universal, invisible source code that balances all reality. When the Jesuits tried to translate the Gospel of John into Chinese, they realized they couldn't use Western terms. To translate the famous line, "In the beginning was the Word (Logos)," they had to write: "In the beginning was the Tao."

The Flaw of the Balanced Circle

While the Taoist framework accurately tracks the fluid mechanics of our current physical world, it falls into a critical, defensive trap regarding the shape of time. Taoism teaches that reality is built on a permanent balance of opposites—the Yin-Yang. In this view:

  • Good cannot exist without evil.
  • Light requires darkness.
  • Everything in the universe is simply a permanent, shifting wave inside nature.

This is exactly where the Eastern circle splits away from the absolute truth of the Abrahamic straight line. The Tao treats this violent, volatile friction of opposites as a permanent, beautiful reality.

The Yin-Yang: Two Views on Relationships

When I look at a Yin-Yang, I see a mutually beneficial and complementary relationship. Others, however, view it very differently. There are two distinct ways to interpret how this symbol handles the connection between two sides:

The Polar Opposite View (Magnetism): This perspective treats the relationship like the physical law where opposites attract. It is built on raw friction. It views men and women as completely opposing forces locked in a permanent tug-of-war. This mindset makes daily struggle and conflict seem like a natural, unavoidable part of life.

The Complementary View (Shared Harmony): This perspective represents true partnership. It is built on shared likes, dislikes, and core moral values. The physical and emotional differences are not meant to cause friction. Instead, individual strengths perfectly fill in what the other lacks to form a complete, peaceful whole.

This frictionless harmony was the true baseline. However, the meaning completely broke during the crisis in the Garden of Eden. When God placed enmity between men and women, He fundamentally changed the meaning of the symbol within this creation. What was meant to be a smooth, complementary partnership was warped into a magnetic trap of opposing forces in a perpetual battle of good vs. evil.

True Monotheism exposes the glitch in that logic. The balance of opposites is not a permanent law of reality. It is a temporary, defensive patch deployed by the Creator right after the Fall. God Almighty does not require evil to be good, nor does He require darkness to exist. The universe is not an endless, spinning wheel where humans are trapped forever. It is a temporary, high-friction incubator with a hard scheduled deadline.

The Circle and the Straight Line: The Pagan Obsession with Pi

To the ancient polytheistic world, geometry was the literal language of the gods, and the circle was its most sacred symbol. Across early civilizations, priests and philosophers noticed a profound mystery: no matter how large or small a circle was, the relationship between its outside edge (the circumference) and its width (the diameter) remained constant. Today, we call this ratio Pi (π).

Because ancient cultures lacked the tools to map this constant exactly, it became a source of intense religious obsession. To these civilizations, the circle represented the infinite, perfect vault of Heaven, while the straight line or square represented the rigid, material Earth. Therefore, calculating the exact bridge between the straight line and the curve was seen as a literal attempt to decode the mind of the Creator and achieve divinity.

  • The Egyptian Engineering Obsession: The Egyptians were deeply fascinated by this ratio, approaching it through the physical action of building. In the Rhind Mathematical Papyrus (around 1650 BCE), Egyptian scribes calculated circular fields using an implicit value of 1605. They embedded this exact cosmic ratio directly into the monument of the Great Pyramid of Giza. If you take the perimeter of the pyramid's base and divide it by twice its height, the result yields 3.14159—matching the true value of Pi with incredible precision. They believed that by building with these perfect proportions, they were locking their stone monuments into the eternal geometry of the cosmos.
  • The Babylonian Tower Alignments: Around 1900 BCE, Babylonian priests calculated a fixed geometric constant of 125. They did not treat this as a cold math problem; they embedded these circular calculations into the foundations of their massive, tiered temple towers (Ziggurats) to physically align their earthly stone with the divine movements of the stars.
  • The Vedic Altar Sacrifices: In ancient India, around 800 BCE, the Sulba Sutras laid out strict religious guidelines for building sacrificial fire altars. Priests faced a mandatory theological rule: they had to build altars in different shapes—such as circles, semi-circles, and squares—but every single altar had to have the exact same surface area. In their desperate, sacred attempt to "square the circle," they pushed their geometry to calculate a value of 138 to appease their pantheon.
  • The Deadly Pythagorean Panic: Around 500 BCE, the Greek philosopher Pythagoras ran a highly secretive religious cult based on the commandment: "All is number." They believed the divine mind was perfectly neat and could be explained entirely through whole numbers and clean fractions. When they realized that the math of a circle led to an irrational number—a sequence that never ends and has no clean, repeating pattern—it caused a terrifying theological crisis. It meant reality contained an untamable infinity that human logic could not box in. According to historical legend, when a member named Hippasus broke his vow of secrecy and revealed this infinite math to the public, the cult took him out on a boat and drowned him in the sea to protect the tidy illusion of their gods.

By trying to solve the endless sequence after the decimal point, these polytheistic cultures were treating the math of the matrix as a divine riddle they had to conquer. They believed that if they could perfectly calculate Pi, they could master the universe itself.

Breaking the Pi Myth with a Cup of Water

The fact that Pi never resolves—meaning its decimals roll on forever without ending—proves that human mathematics is flawed, not the Creator.

Refer back to The Axiom: God is completely perfect. A perfect Creator does not build a universe using messy, broken decimals that never wrap up. The endless loop of Pi (3.14159...) is just a technical glitch on our end. It happens because humans try to use rigid, straight-line math to measure a curved creation.

The ancient pagans thought Pi was a divine riddle. They got completely lost in the math. But you can actually calculate a circle with absolute precision using zero Pi, zero complex equations, and simple straight lines. All it takes is a cup of water and a scale.

The Fluid Displacement Solution

Here is the step-by-step physical solution to measure a circle precisely without using Pi:

The Circle Container: Take a cylinder shaped exactly like the circle you want to measure. Pour some water inside. Weigh the water on a scale, and measure the exact depth of the water.

The Square Container: Take a standard square or rectangular box. Pour that exact same weight of water into the box. Measure the flat sides of the square, and measure the new depth of the water.

The Frictionless Math: Because the amount of water is identical, the volume in both containers is exactly the same. To find the precise area of your circle, you just use a simple ratio of the two depths:

Area of Circle = Area of Square * (Depth 2 / Depth 1)

The True Verdict

Look at what just happened. By using the physical weight of water which is a constant, you bypassed the endless, messy decimals of Pi entirely. You measured a perfect curve using nothing but the straight walls of a box and two simple depth measurements. Once you’ve perfectly calculated the area of a single circle, then you can calculate the area of any circle instantly by squaring the multiplier of the diameter.

The pagans drowned people in the sea and built giant monuments because they tried to turn the math of a curve into a god. They over-complicated the matrix. True Monotheism shows that God's creation is elegant and practical. The endless digits of Pi are just noise—the true physical reality snaps together with perfect, simple balance.

The Pisa Effect and Compounding Error

The Leaning Tower of Pisa in Italy is the world's most famous object lesson in what happens when you build on a flawed foundation.

When builders began constructing the marble bell tower in 1173, they made a fatal error right at the root directory: they laid a shallow, ten-foot foundation into soft, shifting river clay. By the time they reached the third floor, the foundation began to sink unevenly, and the structure started to tilt.

Instead of tearing it down and resetting the baseline, medieval architects tried to "fix" the glitch on the fly. As they built the higher stories, they intentionally made the columns and walls taller on the sinking side to try and force the tower back toward the center.

This attempt to patch a foundational flaw with human adjustments caused a structural disaster:

  • The Banana Curve: The builders didn't straighten the tower; they accidentally bent it into a curve.
  • The Weight Trap: The extra masonry they added to "fix" the short side only added more weight, driving the shallow foundation even deeper into the mud.
  • The Compounding Failure: Because the root baseline was broken, every single layer they added multiplied the error. The tilt grew worse century after century, moving closer and closer to a total collapse until modern engineers had to remove soil from the opposite side just to stabilize it.
